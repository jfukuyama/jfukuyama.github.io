STAT-S 481/681: Practice midterm question

1. Translate a data-generating story into a model and explain its assumptions

For each question in this section, write down a probability model using random variables and their distributions or conditional distributions and identify which variables are observed. You may define additional variables as needed. A deterministic observation or update rule should also be written explicitly.

G1

We collect ten seeds from each of twenty plants and record whether each seed germinates. Let \(p_j\) be the germination probability for seeds from plant \(j\), and let \(G_{ji}\) indicate whether seed \(i\) from plant \(j\) germinates. For this question, draw each \(p_j\) independently to be either \(0.2\) or \(0.8\), with equal probability. Given \(p_j\), each of that plant’s ten seeds germinates independently with probability \(p_j\). Write the model for \(p_j\) and for \(G_{ji}\mid p_j\) in distribution notation.

G2

A rain gauge is read once a day for 60 days. The gauge holds at most 30 mm of water: on a day with more rain than that, it overflows, and the reading is recorded as “full.” Otherwise the reading is the day’s rainfall. Let \(L_i\) be the actual rainfall on day \(i\). Suppose \(L_i\) is drawn independently from a rainfall distribution \(F_\theta\) with the same fixed parameter \(\theta\) for every day. Write the distributional statement for \(L_1,\ldots,L_{60}\) and define a random variable \(R_i\) for what is actually recorded, as a function of \(L_i\).

G3

A GPS collar records how far an animal moves in each hour. The animal is either resting (0) or foraging (1). Let \(Z_t\) denote its unobserved behavior in hour \(t\), and let \(Y_t\) be the distance (in meters) recorded for that hour. The animal starts resting, \(Z_1=0\). At each subsequent hour, it keeps its current behavior with probability \(0.8\) and switches with probability \(0.2\). Given \(Z_t=0\), draw \(Y_t\) from an Exponential distribution with mean 10; given \(Z_t=1\), draw it from an Exponential distribution with mean 200. Conditional on the complete behavior sequence, distances in different hours are independent. Write the initial-state rule and the conditional distributions of \(Z_t\mid Z_{t-1}\) and \(Y_t\mid Z_t\). Mark which quantities would be generated in a simulation but would not ordinarily appear in the observed dataset.

G4

In a capture–recapture study, the lake contains \(N\) fish. Researchers visit the lake twice. On the first visit, they tag and release every fish they catch; on the second visit, they record which of the fish they catch are tagged. To allow some fish to be easier to catch, assign each fish a type \(C_i\), either “easy” or “hard,” independently at the start; each fish has probability \(0.2\) of being easy. On each visit, independently of other fish and of the other visit, an easy fish is caught with probability \(0.3\) and a hard fish with probability \(0.1\). Let \(A_i\) and \(B_i\) indicate whether fish \(i\) is caught on the first and second visits.

Write the distribution of \(C_i\) and the conditional distributions of \(A_i\) and \(B_i\) given \(C_i\). Define what is recorded for a fish caught on the second visit.

2. Assess informative features and identifiability

F1

An on/off sensor records \(X_t\in\{0,1\}\) at each of \(T\) times. Consider this model: \(X_1\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\); at each later time, independently, the sensor keeps its previous value with probability \(q\) and switches to the other value with probability \(1-q\). The parameter \(q\) describes persistence. Three simulated records with \(T=20\) at each of two values of \(q\) are:

\(q\) Simulated records, \(X_1\) through \(X_{20}\)
0.9 00000000001111111111
0.9 11111111111111111111
0.9 00000011110011111100
0.3 01010010101010010110
0.3 01011010001011010101
0.3 01000010010110001110

You observe one record of length \(T\) and want to estimate \(q\) by simulated method of moments. A proposed summary is the mean, that is, the fraction of ones, \(s(X)=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T X_t\): for each candidate \(q\), simulate many length-\(T\) records and compare their average \(s(X)\) with the observed \(s(X)\). Is the mean an informative summary for \(q\) in this model? Explain. Propose an additional summary for fitting \(q\) and describe how you would use it.

F2

Two known parent plants grow at positions 2 and 8 along a straight transect. Each releases four seeds. A seed lands at its parent’s position plus an independent offset drawn uniformly between \(-r\) and \(r\), where \(r\) is an unknown dispersal range. The researcher observes the eight landing positions but not which parent produced each seed. Illustrative simulations give:

\(r\) Eight observed landing positions, in order along the transect
0.3 1.8, 1.9, 2.1, 2.2, 7.8, 7.9, 8.1, 8.2
2 0.5, 1.2, 2.9, 3.7, 6.3, 7.2, 8.8, 9.5

Suggest a numerical feature of the observed landing positions that might help estimate \(r\). Explain how it uses the known parent positions without requiring the parent of each seed to be observed. Would the mean landing position be useful? What would you examine across repeated simulated datasets before choosing your feature?

Here \(k\) controls the rate of improvement. Three simulated success (1) or failure (0) sequences at each of two values of \(k\) are:

0.03 0000000000

You observe one person’s ten outcomes and want to estimate \(k\). Suggest a feature or two that you could calculate on the observed and simulated sequences. What aspect of these outputs motivates your choices? Would the time of the first success alone be a reliable summary? How would you check that the apparent difference is not merely chance variation among these few runs?

F4

Show both its typical value and its run-to-run variability. Then draw a less useful summary and explain the difference.

3. Identify randomness and interpret uncertainty

U1

A call center records the times between 40 consecutive incoming calls. A model says these times are independent draws from an Exponential distribution with rate \(\lambda\) (calls per minute), and \(\lambda\) is estimated by \(\hat\lambda=1/\bar{y}\), where \(\bar{y}\) is the average observed time between calls. For this dataset, \(\hat\lambda=0.8\).

If you knew the true \(\lambda\), how could you simulate the sampling distribution of \(\hat\lambda\)? What does the parametric bootstrap substitute when the true parameter is unknown? Explain why the resulting distribution might be useful and give one reason it might be misleading.

U2

A biologist counts the cells in a growing bacterial colony every hour for the first six hours. In the model, each cell divides at rate \(b\) and dies at rate \(d\), and the two rates are estimated from the counts. The figure shows a bootstrap scatterplot of the estimates \((\hat b,\hat d)\).

What does this tell you about the uncertainties associated with \(b\) and \(d\)? Does the plot show any other quantities we might want to try to interpret?

4. Evaluate models and inferential procedures

E1

A field biologist counts the eggs in each of 150 bird nests. A Poisson model was fitted to these counts. The figure shows the observed counts and three datasets of 150 counts simulated from the fitted model.

Describe how the observed data differ from the simulated datasets. Propose a statistic that captures this difference, and describe how you would use simulations to assess it.

E2

A fitted model gives a very narrow bootstrap distribution for its parameter estimate, but systematically fails to reproduce long waiting times. Is there a contradiction? Explain what the bootstrap result and the failed check each tell you.

E3

A model for how many minutes deliveries are late was fitted by matching the median and the 90th percentile of 100 observed delays. The figure shows the distributions of these two summaries across 1000 datasets simulated from the fitted model; the red lines mark the observed values.

A student concludes that the model fits well. Evaluate this conclusion and suggest an additional check.

E4

A statistic has almost the same distribution at every parameter value allowed by a model. A collaborator says it is useless. Is that necessarily true? Explain separately whether it might be useful for parameter estimation and for model checking.
